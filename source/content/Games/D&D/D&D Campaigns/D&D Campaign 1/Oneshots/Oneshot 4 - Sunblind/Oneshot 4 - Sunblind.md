## Premise

Hobb Barleycorn's paid grain order is sitting in Dunmore's Mill in the farming town of **Gildenfield**, and his carter, Wendel Pike, went to collect it two days ago and never came back. When the party arrives at midday, every door is barred, every window is painted black, and the only person on the streets is a kindly priest lighting lanterns in full sun. Something nobody can see hunts Gildenfield by daylight. The party has one day and one night to get the grain out, find out what is happening, and survive a noon where the light itself is the enemy.

## Themes

- **Inverted horror:** light is danger, darkness is safety; the sun is not a comfort here
- **Atmospheric dread lifted by levity:** a frightened town with gallows humour, booby-trapped hallways and a midnight market
- **Grief and loss:** a widow who lost her husband at noon, a carter who never came home, a graveyard filling too fast
- **Good intentions gone wrong:** a priest who loved his town too much to let its miracle leave
- **Trust and outsiders:** a wary town that has learned to check whether strangers are "real"
- **Body horror (mild):** sunburn, peeling skin, milky eyes, a young man whose skin glows in the dark; never graphic
- **Death and proper burial:** the dead must be buried a certain way before dawn, or they rise

## Hooks

- **Primary — Guild contract:** Hobb Barleycorn, a nervous Waterdeep grain merchant, files a contract with the guild: collect his paid order from Dunmore's Mill in Gildenfield and bring back a credible explanation of what's wrong there. **Reward: 350 gp on delivery of the grain, +100 gp bonus for a full account of what is happening.** He'd also very much like to know what happened to his carter, Wendel.
- **Alternative — Missing person:** Wendel Pike's sister petitions the guild directly. Wendel wrote that he'd be home "by sundown, two days back," and the road from Gildenfield is only half a day.
- **Alternative — Temple concern:** A Waterdeep priest of Lathander notes that Gildenfield's sun temple, usually a model congregation, has stopped sending its tithes and letters since midsummer, and asks the guild to check quietly before raising it with the temple hierarchy.

## Acts

## Possible Endings

- **Full Victory:** The grain is loaded at dusk, Wendel is buried properly, Tam Fennimore is treated, the party has Aldric's journal and has heard his lucid plea, and the town trusts them. Hobb pays in full plus the bonus, and Gildenfield's townsfolk pool their savings to hire the guild to finish the job (leads to [[Oneshot 5 - The Answered Prayer]]).
- **Partial Victory:** The grain gets out, but something slipped: Wendel rose at dawn, the party forced doors by day and lost the town's trust, or they never found the journal. Hobb pays the base reward; the town's follow-up contract still comes, but cooler and with fewer allies on side.
- **Failure / Pyrrhic:** The party abandons the mill at noon or flees Gildenfield. The grain is lost (no reward), and the glarewights' numbers grow. The town still sends for the guild, but the party returns to a worse situation in Oneshot 5.

## DM Notes

**Emotional shape:** unease → dread → understanding → defiance. Act 1 is eerie and disorienting: an empty town in bright sun, a too-friendly priest, enemies nobody can see. Act 2 is warmer, with candlelight, gallows humour and night markets, but each friendly conversation reveals something worse. Act 3 turns the dread into tactics: the party now *knows how to fight in the dark*, and the noon siege should feel like a test they've studied for.

**The core lesson is the reward.** Glarewights are immune to ordinary weapons in bright light, resistant in dim light, and fully vulnerable in darkness (see [[Glarewight]]). The first ambush should feel unfair for one or two rounds, and then the players should *work it out*: shade, doorways, flour, the cat. Never tell them the rule outright. Describe the blade passing through heat haze in the sun, catching and dragging in the awning's shade, and biting deep in a dark doorway. Spell damage (Eldritch Blast, etc.) always works, so nobody is ever helpless. When a player first drags a wight into a cellar and kills it, make it a moment.

**Radiant damage heals glarewights.** Rook's Celestial spells (Guiding Bolt, Sacred Flame, Daylight) feed them. Let him learn it once, then have [[Captain]] react (a swat at the casting hand, a pointed stare). Eldritch Blast (force) and necrotic damage are his real tools here.

**Describe radiant damage as bleaching, not burning.** Wood turns silver-white and paper-light; cloth fades bone-white; stone takes a glassy kiln sheen; flesh peels and goes pale; it smells of hot stone and sun-dried linen, never smoke. The signature image: a wall bleached white except for a person's silhouette.

**Sun sickness (Constitution drain)** recovers on a long rest or with [[Mara Thatch]]'s remedy, so it is resource pressure, not a lasting penalty. A PC dies only if Constitution reaches 0, which takes many failed saves; if a PC ever drops below 6 Constitution, have the symptoms scream it (glowing skin, unquenchable thirst) and have an NPC say plainly "you need to get out of the sun."

**Levity beats to lean on:** Hobb's panicked contract, the ball-bearing hallways, Cordelia applying noble etiquette to a town where "morning tea" is at midnight, Dorrie Nightjar's gallows humour, the cat being right about everything, and Rindle discovering that the sunburn remedy is essentially a terrible tea.

**Common derailments:**
- **The party attacks Aldric at noon in Act 1:** He doesn't fight back. He smiles sadly, and the glarewights in the square defend him (run [[Cooper's Row Ambush]] immediately in the square). He walks away unharmed; anything that hits him leaves a pale, glassy mark and he doesn't seem to notice. He isn't meant to be fought until [[Oneshot 5 - The Answered Prayer]].
- **The party forces doors by day:** Every forced door costs town trust (see [[The Lantern Inspection]]). Run [[Breaking In]] if they want the fight.
- **The party just grabs the grain and leaves on day 1:** The mill is bolted and the miller's family is in the cellar beneath it; they won't open until dusk. Hobb's wagon is gone (the horse fled). The party needs the town's help to move the grain, and that requires Act 2.
- **The party skips the burial:** Wendel rises at dawn; run the combat option in [[Before Dawn]].

**Rook and Captain:** Captain is the warlock's celestial patron in disguise, and the deva-light in Gildenfield is something Captain knows and grieves. See [[Captain]] for reactions to use throughout. Give Rook's player quiet, private moments of warmth and grief near the temple.

**Brok** is not in this adventure (his player is the DM). If he joins in a future replay, his mirror-polished armour is perfect: glarewights reflected in it show up as faint heat haze.

**Tracking into Oneshot 5:** Note at the end of the session whether Wendel was buried, whether Tam was treated, whether the party has the journal, whether the town trusts them, and each PC's current Constitution loss. A long rest between sessions restores Constitution.
